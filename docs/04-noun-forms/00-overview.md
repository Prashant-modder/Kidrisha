## Genders

### Masculine

Adult human males and biologically male animals.

> **Examples:** man, king, father, bull, stallion.

### Feminine

Adult human females and biologically female animals.

> **Examples:** woman, queen, mother, cow, mare.

### Neuter, Inanimate, Abstract

Non-living physical items, everyday concepts, states of being, and ideas.

> **Examples:** stone, table, freedom, happiness, sleep, weather.

### Children

Below teenagers; teenagers not included. Human babies, toddlers, and young kids up to roughly age 12. Once they hit 13, they graduate grammatically into Masculine or Feminine.

> **Examples:** infant, toddler, child, little boy, little girl.

### Collective, Places

Geographic locations, cities, rooms, or any group of distinct items operating as a single unit.

> **Examples:** forest, village, bedroom, ocean, a flock of birds, a pack of wolves, a crowd.

### Metaphysical, Sapient, Non-organic

Thinking, self-aware entities that are not made of organic flesh and blood, including advanced technology or philosophical constructs.

> **Examples:** artificial intelligence/robots, sentient holograms, pure thought entities, clones without souls.

### Supernatural, Cosmic

Gods, cosmic forces, celestial bodies, and magical or religious entities that exist beyond regular human reality.

> **Examples:** god, demon, ghost, the sun, the moon, a black hole, destiny, time itself.

## Forms (Cases)

### Core Structure

**Nominative** — marks the doer of the action.
> *Example:* "[does something]Boy eats rice."

**Accusative** — marks the receiver of the action.
> *Example:* "Boy eats [does something to]rice."

**Genitive** — shows ownership, relationship, or connection between nouns.
> *Example:* "[of the]Boy pen"

### Relational Cases

**Instrumental** — shows the tool, means, or method used to complete an action.
> *Example:* "Boy cuts fruit [using/by means of]knife"

**Dative** — marks the indirect object: the person receiving a gift, benefit, or purpose ("for").
> *Example:* "The teacher gives a book to the [for himself]boy."

**Comitative** — means "accompanied by" or "along with" someone. No postposition needed.
> *Example:* "Boy cuts fruit [alongside/company of]us"

**Abessive** — means "lacking", "in the absence of", or "without" something.
> *Example:* "Boy cuts fruit [without the boy/bereft of]us"

### Spatial Cases

**Locative** — shows static location or time ("at", "in", or "on").
> *Example:* "Boy [at/in somewhere]house"

**Allative** — shows physical movement towards a destination or place.
> *Example:* "Boy moves [toward/in direction of]Taj Mahal"

**Ablative** — shows general source, origin, or movement away from a starting point.
> *Example:* "Boy moves [away/in opposite direction of]Taj Mahal"

**Elative** — shows specific movement out from the inside of an enclosed space.
> *Example:* "Boy is [outside/out from in somewhere]house"

## Numbers

| Number | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | Exactly one (1) noun/pronoun | "[One] Boy" |
| Dual/Trial | Exactly two (2) or three (3) | "[Two/Three] Boy" |
| Plural | Four or more (4+) | "[Four+] Boy" |
